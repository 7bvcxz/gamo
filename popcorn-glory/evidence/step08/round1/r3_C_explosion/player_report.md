1. Timeline
- 0:00: First look. I saw a popcorn machine inside a faint circle, a coin counter at 0, a row of 8 empty circles, and a bottom panel showing a grey bucket, "25" and an up arrow. There were also back, sound and restart buttons. My guess was that tapping the machine makes popcorn and earns coins, and the panel is an upgrade I couldn't afford yet.
- 0:10: I tapped the machine. A corn kernel dropped into the first circle and the hint circle disappeared, so I understood each tap loads one kernel.
- 0:27: After 8 taps the row filled, emptied and gave me 10 coins. A yellow bar started filling the bottom panel. I read it as progress toward the upgrade.
- 1:11: At 30 coins the panel turned bright yellow and the arrow turned red. It clearly meant "you can buy this now".
- 1:20: I bought the bucket for 25. The machine got a striped awning and bunting. The next upgrade was a cart for 64.
- 1:40: One batch now paid 22 coins, and popcorn flew out across the floor.
- 1:52: I tried holding my finger down to see if it would keep feeding kernels. It only loaded one, the same as a tap.
- 2:25: At 71 coins I bought the cart. The machine got wheels and there was a big burst of popcorn. A clock badge reading "3:03" appeared under the coins. The next upgrade was a bigger cart for 163.
- 2:44: That batch paid 48. The clock had gone from 3:03 to 3:21, so it counts up, not down.
- 3:00: Time was up at about 55+ coins, still short of 163.

2. What felt good and what didn't
- Good: I understood the core loop within 10 seconds without reading a word. The kernel row was a clear counter, and the yellow fill bar plus the panel lighting up made "save up, then buy" obvious. Every upgrade visibly changed the machine (awning, then wheels), and payouts roughly doubled each time (10, 22, 48). The flying popcorn after upgrades was satisfying.
- Not so good: Each tap does very little, so the middle stretch felt like a chore of 8 taps per batch. Holding the finger down did nothing extra, so there was no way to tap less.

3. What confused me
- The clock badge: It showed up at 3:03 rather than 0:00 and counted up. I couldn't tell if it was a run timer, a goal, or something I was racing against.
- Upgrade preview: I didn't know what an upgrade would give before buying it. I found out by doing it.
- Unused buttons: I never tried the restart or back buttons because I was afraid of losing progress.
- Late game: I couldn't see any goal or end state beyond the next upgrade.

4. Would I keep playing?
Probably for a few more minutes, to see what the 163 upgrade looks like and what the clock means. The visual upgrades are the hook. If the tapping doesn't get more interesting soon (automation, combos, or a reason for the clock), I'd likely put it down. Doubling the payout keeps the cost per upgrade about the same, so it starts to feel repetitive.
