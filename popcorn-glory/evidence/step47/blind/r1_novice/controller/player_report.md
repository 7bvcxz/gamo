I played the popcorn shop game for the full three minutes. My report as the player:

**1. Timeline**
- **0:00** First look: a popcorn shop with three bands. Customers at a red counter at the top, a kitchen with a hopper and crane in the middle, and a red popcorn machine inside a red/yellow/blue ring at the bottom. Under the machine was a row of empty circles, beside it some locked slots, and at the bottom a "30 coins, +4 ▸ +6" upgrade. Before touching anything I guessed: tap the machine to pop, and the ring is a timing gauge.
- **0:18** Tapped the machine. A dot ran around the ring and a puff of popcorn floated up through the crane to a customer. I had **8 coins**, and a yellow bar in the upgrade panel started filling toward 30.
- **0:36–0:47** Tapped again: +8 more (16). It worked, but I couldn't tell yet whether timing mattered.
- **1:03** Tried a 2-second hold. The release marker stopped past the yellow, at the edge of the red, and one small circle turned gold.
- **1:15** Held for 1.6 s and landed in the yellow. Popcorn burst out with confetti, three circles were gold, and I had 25 coins. **Figured it out: holding cooks, and letting go in the yellow is a perfect batch.**
- **1:28** Another 1.6 s perfect. 45 coins, 5 gold circles, and the upgrade panel lit up with a red arrow.
- **1:37** Bought the upgrade. The machine got a striped awning and popcorn rained down. The next one cost 54. The counter sign changed from a crossed-out cup to a striped bucket, and the kitchen started to glow.
- **1:52–2:03** More perfects. At 9 of 9 gold circles came a huge explosion, the circles reset, a dot lit on the small board by the counter, and the kitchen began filling striped buckets.
- **2:15** Tapped the kitchen. It became the selected station: a side card showed its stats (4.0 s → 3.0 s, bucket 15 → 20) and the bottom bar offered a kitchen upgrade for 250. My coins went up while I wasn't cooking, so the kitchen runs on its own.
- **2:29–2:39** Tapped the machine's icon to select it again and bought the 54 upgrade.
- **2:51** The machine is now a cart on wheels. Another perfect, next upgrade 97, and a faded customer was appearing at the counter when time ran out.

**2. What felt good, and what didn't**
- **Good:**
  - Finding the hold-and-release timing with no text at all was very satisfying.
  - The yellow perfect burst and the gold circles filling toward a big payoff gave a clear "do it again" loop.
  - Upgrades visibly change the machine (awning, then wheels), and the panel filling with yellow and lighting up told me when I could buy.
- **Less good:**
  - Plain taps paid the same 8 coins as early on, so the first two taps taught me nothing about the ring.
  - Coin jumps were hard to read: after the first upgrade I had 25, not the 15 I expected, because the kitchen's sales mixed in.

**3. What confused me**
- I never learned what the little board next to the counter sign is for (dots lighting up, plus stars under it), or why the sign kept switching between a crossed-out cup and a bucket.
- I don't know what the locked dashed slots beside the machine are for.
- The side card's star and circular-arrow numbers (500 → 550, 400 → 350) meant nothing to me.
- I didn't know at first that tapping a station picks which one the bottom upgrade applies to. I found it by accident when I tapped the kitchen.
- Near the end, buckets piled up in the kitchen with no customers at the counter. I couldn't tell whether I was supposed to do something about that.
- I never tried the top buttons (sparkle, sound, restart, the newspaper icon, the window icon).

**4. Would I keep playing?**
Yes, for a while. The timing hold has a real skill feel, the upgrades come quickly, and the kitchen and locked slots promise more to unlock. My worry is that once 1.6 s becomes muscle memory, holding the same length every time could get repetitive unless the yellow zone moves or shrinks with upgrades.
