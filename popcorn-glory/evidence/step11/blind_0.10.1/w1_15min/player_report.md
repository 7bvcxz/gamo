I played the full fifteen minutes, and I used only the play commands and the screenshots they printed. The tool's last message said "the three minutes are over" even though the session was fifteen minutes.

1. Timeline
- 0:00–0:11 The first screen showed a red popcorn machine inside a ring (blue, then yellow, then red), a coin counter at 0, a little person standing on a red shelf at the top, and an upgrade card (30 coins, +10 → +16). I guessed I should press the machine, maybe when the ring was in the yellow.
- 0:14 A quick tap gave +1 and sent a striped box sliding along the shelf. A needle appeared at 12 o'clock.
- 0:36–1:05 Holding for 2 s gave +5 and the needle stopped in the red. Holding 3 s made black smoke and paid only +1 (burnt). I worked out that the needle sweeps clockwise while I hold: blue is raw, yellow is perfect, red is burnt. A 1.6 s hold gave +10 with a golden popcorn burst. That was the moment it clicked.
- 1:28 At 37 coins the card turned bright yellow with a red arrow, which clearly meant "buy me". I bought it and the machine got bunting.
- 2:08 The second upgrade turned the machine into a cart on wheels.
- 2:26 Tapping the customer counted as a press on the machine. The whole screen is the button.
- 3:01 At the third upgrade a second customer arrived, plus a hand icon with left/right arrows and a crank on the cart. A single swipe moved the needle only a little (+4). Swiping back and forth about 4 times hit the yellow and paid +52 instead of +40, a 30% bonus. A plain hold still paid +40.
- 4:24 After the star upgrade a blue gear card appeared (260, "x1.3"). Once I bought it, a motor turned the crank for me, so a plain hold got the bonus and hand-cranking added nothing.
- 5:15 A helper-cart card appeared (1,200, +19 every 3 s).
- 6:14 Three flavour jars appeared. Yellow x1.0 had the normal window. Brown x1.5 had a later yellow window and burned quickly: 2.0 s burned it and 1.85 s was perfect. Orange x1.35 had a huge window: 1.2 s was too early and paid half. I chose brown and held it for 1.8 s each time from then on.
- 7:40 A third customer arrived. 8:53 I bought the helper, a mini-cart with its own timer ring. It added roughly 250 coins every 8 s while I did nothing.
- 9:59 / 11:18 / 12:58 I bought the 2,500 upgrade (two kettles), the 5,800 one (gold wheels) and the 14,000 one (lamp posts and a fourth customer). Perfect pops reached +1,341.
- 13:20 The back arrow opened a menu of four modes with no words: Star, A, B and C. Mode A turned out to be a plain tapper: +1 per tap, and holding did nothing extra.
- 14:06 When I went back into Star, all my progress was gone and I was at 0 coins with the starting machine. I rebuilt a little until the time ran out.

2. What was fun and what wasn't
Fun: working out the hold timing with no instructions, and the first perfect golden burst; the cart changing shape at each upgrade, and new customers joining the queue; the crank as a new move that paid more; choosing a flavour for risk against reward -- caramel's tight window was a real decision.
Less fun: buying the gear quietly removed the crank skill, and that was the most hands-on part. After about minute 8 it was the same 1.8 s hold over and over. Each upgrade went from needing about 3 pops to about 12, so the main reward was just bigger numbers.

3. What confused me
- I never understood what the shelf and customers are for. Boxes sometimes fell off below the shelf, and I couldn't tell if that mattered.
- "x1.3" on the gear card didn't say whether it was my current bonus or what I would get.
- The orange jar's clock icon meant nothing I could find.
- Any touch anywhere starts a pop, so tapping the helper or a customer wasted a pop.
- The "14,000" price overlapped the up arrow on the card.
- Leaving through the back arrow wiped my run without any warning. That was the worst moment.
- I only found the four-mode menu by accident in the last two minutes.

4. Would I keep playing past 15 minutes?
Probably not on Star. The timing core was satisfying, but once it was learned it became routine. Losing my progress by pressing back would make me reluctant to invest again. I would try modes B and C out of curiosity, since the menu suggests each one plays differently.

6. What I think the goal is
Run a growing popcorn stand. Pop each batch perfectly by releasing in the yellow window, earn coins from a growing queue of customers, and upgrade the machine into a bigger and fancier cart, adding automation and flavours as you go. It is an incremental idle game built around one timing skill. The four-mode menu suggests it may also be comparing different versions of that idea.
