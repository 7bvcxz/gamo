1. Timeline
- 0:00–0:16 The first screen showed a red popcorn machine inside a ring of red, yellow and blue, a small customer on a counter line at the top, and an upgrade card ("30, +10 > +16"). I guessed the ring was a timing gauge and tapped the machine. I got +1, and a striped box slid toward the customer.
- 0:33–0:50 I tried holding my finger down instead. A needle sweeps clockwise while you hold and the popcorn pops when you let go. Holding 2 s ended in the red and paid +5. Holding 1.6 s ended in the yellow and paid +10, with golden popcorn spraying everywhere. By then I understood the colors: blue is undercooked, yellow is perfect, red is burning and the smoke at the top is burnt.
- 1:07–2:17 The upgrade card lit up bright yellow with a red arrow when I could afford it. I bought three upgrades in a row (30, 54, 97). Each one made the machine bigger: first an awning, then wheels, then lights. The yellow zone also got wider.
- 2:17 The third upgrade was a big moment: sparkles, a second customer, and a round knob with a red handle on the side. Tapping or dragging the knob did nothing special. It just counted as a short press on the machine (+1, +5).
- 3:09 I let go early on purpose and got exactly half pay (+20 instead of +40). That confirmed what the colors meant.
- 3:33–4:19 A gear card appeared (260, "x1.3"). I bought it and the payout went from 66 to 85, so it multiplies coins. After that, the card turned into a helper offer: "+0 > +19 3.0s" for 1,200.
- 5:11 Three jars appeared on the left: yellow x1.0, brown x1.5 and orange x1.35 with a clock icon. Picking one changes the ring: brown x1.5 moves the yellow later and the burn comes right after it (2.0 s burnt it, +101; 1.8 s was perfect, +202). Orange x1.35 has a huge yellow zone that starts early; it pays a bit less per pop, but each pop is quicker and much safer. I switched to it. One release cut too close to the edge only paid +147, so I aimed for the middle at 1.5 s after that.
- 7:48 I bought the helper. A mini cart in a bubble with its own ring now pays about +50 every 3 s. Coins went from 234 to 484 while I waited hands-off.
- 8:48 The seventh upgrade (2,500) added a third customer and a second kettle in the glass. Perfect pops were worth +470 and the helper rose to +80 automatically.
- 9:09 The back arrow opened a menu of four modes: Star (the one I'd been playing), A, B and C. I tried A. It's a plain tapper: no ring, holding does nothing extra, and you get +1 per tap.

2. What felt satisfying, and what didn't
- Satisfying: finding the hold-and-release move on my own; the golden burst on a perfect pop; the cards filling up like progress bars, then lighting up when I could buy; seeing the cart physically grow with every upgrade; the flavor jars -- they brought back a real choice (risk against speed against payout) just as the game was getting repetitive, and I could read all of it from the ring alone.
- Not satisfying: from about 2 to 5 minutes it was the same 1.6 s hold over and over; the needle never sped up and nothing pushed back. The customers seemed to be decoration; they never left or got impatient. The helper's earnings were easy to miss.

3. What confused me
- The knob with the red handle: I never found out what it does.
- The customers: I couldn't tell whether they matter at all.
- The idle ring: it turns washed-out white or pale when I'm not touching it, and I wasn't sure if that meant something.
- The clock icon on the orange jar: I only figured it out by trying it.
- The modes menu: I only found the other modes by accident through the back arrow, and A started over at 0 coins.

4. Would I keep playing?
Probably for a little while. I'd want to see the next jar or machine feature and try modes B and C. The upgrades reveal new things at a good pace. But if no new twist arrives soon, the core hold-and-release would wear thin.
