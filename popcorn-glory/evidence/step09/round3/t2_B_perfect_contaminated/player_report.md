2. What felt good and what didn't
- Good: finding the hold-and-release mechanic on my own felt great. The release mark stays on the ring after you let go, so I could see exactly how early or late I was and correct the next hold. The bottom card fills up and then turns bright with a red arrow when you can buy -- "buy now" without any words. Each upgrade visibly changes the machine (awning, then wheels), and the popcorn spilling everywhere looks nice. The "+10 ▸ +16" preview made it clear what I was buying.
- Less good: the loop gets repetitive fast: hold, release, repeat. The yellow window is narrow, and the same hold length gave +10 one time and +5 the next, which felt a bit random.

3. What confused me
- A single tap gives 1 coin and only flashes a needle, so at first I thought tapping was the whole game. Nothing on screen hints that you should hold.
- I never worked out what the smoke puff at the top of the ring means. I'm guessing it's a "burnt" warning for the red zone.
- In the idle state the sweet spot is white, and it only turns yellow during and after a hold. At first I wasn't sure it was the same zone.
- I never tried the back, sound or restart buttons, and I didn't find anything else to do besides the one upgrade card.

4. Would I keep playing past three minutes?
Probably for a few more minutes. I'd want to see what the machine turns into next, and the 97-coin upgrade felt reachable in about four pops. But if the only thing ever on offer is that one upgrade card and the same timing ring, I'd lose interest soon. I'd stay longer if a second thing to spend coins on showed up, or if the timing zone changed.
