The ten minutes are up. I got to level 4 and filled two of the four goals for turning my village into a city.

**1. Timeline**
- **00:00–00:20:** Opened on a tiny floating island at night. There was a garden bed of tomatoes, a bunny creature and a prompt saying "Tap the ripe crop". I harvested, sowed and watered by tapping the bed when told. Each day lasts 30 seconds, and the day/night cycle looks lovely.
- **00:26–00:46:** "Tap a Momo." The bunny kept walking away, so my first two taps missed and I only caught it on the third. A panel opened with a pencil button for picking which tiles it looks after.
- **00:54–01:09:** I assigned the farm to it, and from then on it farmed by itself (+$4 a harvest). A large block of hint text then explained building on empty tiles and tearing buildings down. That was a lot to read at once.
- **01:15–01:30:** A hint said I could buy the sea next to the island. I tapped the sea, then tapped again to buy it for $25. A new tile rose up with a tree on it, which felt great.
- **01:39:** I tried to tap the envelope bubble over the bunny, but the tap went to the sea tile behind it ("$35, not enough money"). I opened the Mailbox button instead.
- **01:55–02:28:** Momo writes letters proposing upgrades (wider field, watering less often, giving the soil something back), each with a "Let's research it" button. This is a charming way to introduce research. The research tree is big (Farm, Lumber, Mine, Smelting, Power, Games…) and it told me to build a research lab.
- **02:44–03:04:** Tapped an empty tile and got a build menu: Farm $10, Mine (opens at level 3), Lumberyard $80, Research lab $40, Special. I built the lab, which took about 4 seconds.
- **03:23–03:59:** The lab produced flasks. At 3 of 3 I started Field expansion, then hit level 3 with confetti and the Mine unlocked.
- **04:17–05:13:** Hired a second bunny, Mochi, from the Shop for $50. I assigned it to the lab tile, but its panel kept saying "Free time" and the Assign button seemed to do nothing. I was confused for a while, but later it was clearly working at the lab.
- **05:24:** Started Watering efficiency ($40).
- **05:58:** Special turned out to be landmarks: School (level 4), Clock Tower (level 5), Momo Monument (level 6).
- **06:19–06:56:** Built a lumberyard because the Mine now costs $120 plus 10 wood. Hired a third bunny, Pomu; the button said $80 but the floating number said -$72. Pomu also showed "Free time" for a while before switching to "Working at Lumberyard".
- **07:07:** The Status screen gave me a clear goal: Village → City needs land 9, 3 Momos, 3 research levels and $1.5K of value produced.
- **07:38:** Started Bigger harvests.
- **07:53–09:16:** Bought four more sea tiles ($35, $45, $55, $75). Income reached about $160 a minute. One price label was half hidden behind the lab and a bunny.
- **09:25:** Reached level 4. Land 9/9 and Momo 3/3 were done. Research was 2/3 and value was $790 of $1.5K.

**2. What felt satisfying or fun**
- Land rising out of the sea when I bought it.
- The bunnies' letters suggesting research ideas.
- The bunnies working on their own while the income per minute kept climbing.
- The sky cycling through dawn, sunset and moonlight.
- Confetti on level-ups.
- Having a concrete Village → City checklist to aim for.

**3. What confused me**
- **Moving bunnies:** they are hard to tap because they keep walking.
- **The envelope bubble:** tapping it selected the sea tile behind it instead of opening the letter.
- **"Free time" after assigning:** a bunny still said "Free time" right after I assigned it, and the Assign button gave no visible feedback.
- **Unclear panel lines:** I never understood "Comparing: a few days are needed on each side".
- **Prices:** the price shown and the amount actually spent didn't match ($80 vs -$72, $45 vs -$37). It may be income mixed into the same floating number.
- **Hints:** the long hint texts covered the panels I was supposed to use.
- **Hidden price label:** one buy-prompt label was partly hidden behind buildings.
- **Where to tap for land:** it wasn't obvious which sea spots could be bought; one tap did nothing.

**4. Would I keep playing?** Yes, at least a while longer. I want to finish becoming a city, open the mine and build the School. The loop is cozy, there's always a next thing to do, and the bunnies are endearing. My worry is that once things run on their own, much of the play becomes opening menus and waiting.

**5. Confirmation:** I used only the play commands (look, tap, wait, note, status) and read only the screenshot files they printed. I did not open, list or search any other files, code, docs or the web.
