**2. What felt satisfying or fun**
- The art is lovely: the island, the bunnies, and the sky changing from day to sunset to moonlight.
- Handing my own chores to a Momo and then watching it do them was the best moment. It made me feel I'd built something.
- Research proposals arriving as handwritten letters from the bunnies is charming, and it's a clever way to point at the next upgrade.
- Upgrades come quickly: a new level, unlock, or piece of land every minute or so. The City checklist always gave me a clear next goal.
- Watching the island grow tile by tile toward a target was very satisfying.

**3. What confused me or where I got stuck**
- The "Next: City" goal list only showed up because I tapped a small label out of curiosity. I'd have liked to see it much earlier.
- Mochi at the lab sometimes had a "…" speech bubble. I never found out whether that meant idle, blocked, or just chatting.
- The first "Build a research lab" button opened a menu where the lab was locked. It felt like a dead end.
- The research tree uses two numbers, like "4/3" next to "$20", and I had to guess that the flask number meant flasks I have versus flasks needed.
- Mail bubbles float over the Momos, but tapping one selected the farm under it instead of opening the letter.
- I couldn't tell whether my own taps (Sow/Harvest, Experiment) still mattered once a Momo was assigned, or whether I was just getting in its way.
- With 30-second days, the "Day 20" counter climbs fast and doesn't seem to mean anything.
- The next land tile ended up half off-screen. I only found out by trying that the view can be dragged.

**4. Would I keep playing?**
Yes, at least for a while. I had just reached Lumberyard, Mine and "become a City" all at once, and I wanted to see what a City unlocks and get the Mine going. The cozy look and the bunny letters give it more personality than most idle games. My worry is that once everything is automated, the loop becomes "wait for money, buy the next thing". The letters and new building types would have to keep coming for me to stay.
